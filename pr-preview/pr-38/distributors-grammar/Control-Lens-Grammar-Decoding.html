Description

A language model generates text a piece at a time: given the pieces so far, it weighs each piece of its vocabulary, & ending, & a decoder chooses one. Grammar-constrained decoding masks those weights, so that only pieces which continue a word of a grammar's language may be chosen, & ending only at a word of the language, so the generated text always parses. See Willard & Louf, Efficient Guided Generation for Large Language Models.

A Grammar is a decoder's constraint, as it's a parser, & a printer, & a Transducer, by transducerG, whose Charts are the states of the constraint, scanned a token at a time. decodeG decodes a syntax value, parsing the text which it masks its predictor to generate.

Pieces & tokens

A language model's vocabulary is not its grammar's tokens: models generate pieces of text, by sub-word tokenizers such as byte-pair encoding, each piece a string of many characters, which needn't align with a grammar's lexemes. A Vocabulary is the strings of tokens which its pieces stand for, indexed by position, as a model indexes its vocabulary, & a piece is allowed where a Chart scans its whole string. Masking a vocabulary is thus composing a grammar with the detokenizer, the monoid homomorphism from strings of pieces to strings of tokens, whose inverse image of a context-free language is context-free. See Koo, Liu & He, Automata-based constraints for language model decoding.

allowedPieces scans a vocabulary's pieces together, by a trie, so pieces which share a prefix share its scanning, & a prefix which is a dead end prunes every piece which begins with it.

Many strings of pieces stand for the same text, but a model is trained on only one of them, its tokenizer's canonical one, & weighs the others poorly. A mask allows them all, which can steer a model to text it would've tokenized differently. See Beurer-Kellner, Fischer & Vechev, Guiding LLMs The Right Way: Fast, Non-Invasive Constrained Generation, & Vieira et al., From Language Models over Tokens to Language Models over Characters.

Opportunistic masking

Masking a whole vocabulary each step costs a scan of its trie, while a model usually weighs most what the grammar allows anyway. So decode first lets its Chooser choose from the unmasked weights, & only masks when the choice isn't allowed, choosing again from the masked weights. This decodes exactly as masking each step would: a greedy choice which is allowed is the masked weights' greatest; & a sampled choice is sampled from the masked weights, as a piece i of weight p_i, out of an allowed total of Z, is chosen first with probability p_i, & else, with probability 1 - Z, then with p_i / Z, in all p_i / Z.

Masking is not conditioning

Masking a model's weights a piece at a time decodes from a different distribution than the model's, conditioned on generating a word of the language: masking can't see that an allowed piece leads only to words the model weighs poorly. Decoding from the conditional distribution is intractable in general, & approximated by importance sampling & sequential Monte Carlo. See Park et al., Grammar-Aligned Decoding, & Loula et al., Syntactic and Semantic Control of Large Language Models via Sequential Monte Carlo.

Predictors

A Predictor is any function from the pieces so far to Weights, in a monad, so a language model is wrapped as a predictor by calling it, in IO, & keeping its prompt in a closure; its logits l_i are weights exp l_i, & its end-of-sequence piece is endWeight.

Decode text of a Transducer's language, from a Predictor, by a Chooser, in at most a given number of pieces. Each step, the predictor weighs the pieces of a Vocabulary, & ending; the chooser chooses among them, masked to what the grammar allows; & a chosen piece is scanned, by the Chart of the text so far.

>>> let vocab = vocabulary ["tr", "ue", "true", "false", "f"]
>>> let et = transducerG @Char boolG
>>> let uniform _ = pure (Weights (IntMap.fromList [(i, 1) | i <- [0 .. 4]]) 1)
>>> runIdentity (decode greedy uniform vocab 10 et)
Finished "true"
>>> evalStateT (decode sampled uniform vocab 10 et) (mkStdGen 1)
Finished "false"

A predictor which only ever weighs ending, outside the language, is stuck.

>>> runIdentity (decode greedy (\_ -> pure (Weights IntMap.empty 1)) vocab 10 et)
Stuck ""

data Weights Source #

The weights a Predictor puts on its next choice: on each piece of its Vocabulary, by index, & on ending. Weights are nonnegative & needn't sum to one; a piece missing from pieceWeights has no weight.

Choosers

type Chooser (m :: Type -> Type) = Weights -> m (Maybe Int) Source #

A decoder's choice, among Weights, of the index of a piece, or Nothing, to end. A Chooser should only choose what has weight.

greedy :: Applicative m => Chooser m Source #

Choose what weighs most, preferring to end, & the least index of the pieces which weigh most.

>>> runIdentity (greedy (Weights (IntMap.fromList [(0, 1), (1, 3), (2, 3)]) 2))
Just 1

sampled :: forall g (m :: Type -> Type). (RandomGen g, Monad m) => Chooser (StateT g m) Source #

Sample in proportion to weight, by a random generator.

>>> let weights = Weights (IntMap.fromList [(0, 1), (1, 1)]) 0
>>> evalStateT (traverse (const (sampled weights)) [1 .. 8 :: Int]) (mkStdGen 7)
[Just 0,Just 1,Just 0,Just 1,Just 0,Just 0,Just 0,Just 1]

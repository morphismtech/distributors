PartialIso

type PartialIso s t a b = forall (p :: Type -> Type -> Type) (f :: Type -> Type). (Choice p, Applicative f, Filterable f) => p a (f b) -> p s (f t) Source #

PartialIso is a first class inexhaustive pattern, similar to how Prism is a first class exhaustive pattern, by combining Prisms, constructor patterns, with filterPrisms, filter patterns.

Iso ∪ Prism ∪ filterPrism ⊂ PartialIso ⊂ Bifocal.

Applied to a value, a pattern does one of three things. It matches, focusing on part of the value, to work on & rebuild. Or it doesn't match, & either passes the value through, untouched, as a Prism does, so there is nothing to work on, but nothing goes wrong; or it fails, rejecting the value, as a filter does. A PartialIso may do any of the three.

>>> traverseOf _Just (Just . succ) (Just 1)
Just (Just 2)
>>> traverseOf _Just (Just . succ) Nothing
Just Nothing
>>> traverseOf (satisfied even) (Just . succ) 3
Nothing

Here _Just matches Just 1, & passes Nothing through, while satisfied even fails on 3. Passing through & failing can only be told apart where a result can succeed with nothing done, like traverseOf's; a parser can't, since a value that doesn't match isn't parsed, so it fails.

PartialIsos are the compositions of Prisms, which pass a mismatch through, & filterPrisms, which fail, forwards where they reject what a Prism passes through. Each is a filterPrism after a Prism,

prop> partialMarket build match = filterPrism _Just . prism build (maybe (Left Nothing) match)

& isomorphic to a PartialMarket, so clonePartialIso is the identity.

prop> clonePartialIso pattern = pattern

Its partial functions, by withPartialIso, forget passing through, which withPartialIso pattern partialIso rebuilds from, failing instead.

A PartialIso chooses whether its pattern matches by Choice, & fails in either direction by Filterable, emptying with catMaybes (pure Nothing) & filtering with mapMaybe. So it needs no Cochoice of its own, unlike its action >?< on a Choice & Cochoice partial profunctor, which has no Filterable to fail by.

>>> :{
let halve :: PartialIso' Int Int
    halve = partialIso
      (\n -> if even n then Just (n `div` 2) else Nothing)
      (Just . (* 2))
:}

Forwards, a PartialIso folds at most one focus, so preview, ^?, has & toListOf match by it.

>>> preview halve 6
Just 3
>>> 7 ^? halve
Nothing
>>> has halve 7
False
>>> toListOf halve 6
[3]

Backwards, a PartialIso folds forwards by coPartialIso.

>>> preview (coPartialIso halve) 3
Just 6

withPartialIso converts a PartialIso to both directions.

>>> withPartialIso halve $ \match build -> (match 6, build 3)
(Just 3,Just 6)

Since it may fail to build, as well as to match, a PartialIso modifies by traverseOf with a Filterable Applicative like Maybe, in place of over & set.

>>> traverseOf halve (Just . succ) 6
Just 8
>>> traverseOf halve (Just . succ) 7
Nothing

A Prism is exhaustive though, passing a mismatched source through, as does its clone, where its partial functions, rebuilt by partialIso, fail.

>>> traverseOf _Just (Just . succ) Nothing
Just Nothing
>>> traverseOf (clonePartialIso _Just) (Just . succ) Nothing
Just Nothing
>>> traverseOf (withPartialIso _Just partialIso) (Just . succ) Nothing
Nothing

Neither review, over nor set applies to a PartialIso, since Identity can't fail, so isn't Filterable; & view applies only to a Monoidal focus, as for a Fold, by Const.

PartialIsos compose by ., with each other, & with Isos, Prisms & filterPrisms.

>>> preview (halve . halve) 12
Just 3
>>> preview (_Just . halve) (Just 4)
Just 2
>>> preview (halve . filterPrism _Just) 6
Just (Just 3)

type PartialIso' s a = PartialIso s s a a Source #

A simple PartialIso' s a is an identification of a subset of s with a subset of a.

A proper PartialIso', with partial functions match & build, by withPartialIso, inverts what it matches & what it builds.

prop> match s = Just a  ==>  build a = Just s
prop> build a = Just s  ==>  match s = Just a

However, sometimes an improper PartialIso' will be useful, matching what it builds, but not necessarily rebuilding what it matches.

prop> build a = Just s  ==>  match s = Just a

For an improper PartialIso', norm = build <=< match is an idempotent

prop> norm = norm <=< norm

and can be regarded as a normalization within some equivalence class of terms.

type APartialIso s t a b = PartialMarket a b a (Maybe b) -> PartialMarket a b s (Maybe t) Source #

APartialIso is monomorphically a PartialIso.

prop> PartialIso s t a b ⊂ APartialIso s t a b

data PartialMarket a b s t Source #

A PartialMarket is the concrete form of a PartialIso, isomorphic to it, a Market which may fail in either direction: building backwards, partially; & matching forwards, either its focus, or, where its pattern doesn't match, Nothing, failing, or Just a target, passing through, as a Prism does.

prop> withPartialMarket (partialMarket build match) k = k build match
prop> withPartialMarket pattern partialMarket = pattern

Its functions are those of a Prism with a Maybe t target, a PartialIso failing in its target, rather than in its Filterable, & the conversions are inverse.

prop> withPrism (withPartialMarket pattern prism :: Prism s (Maybe t) a b) partialMarket = (clonePartialIso pattern :: PartialIso s t a b)
prop> withPartialMarket (withPrism prsm partialMarket :: PartialIso s t a b) prism = (clonePrism prsm :: Prism s (Maybe t) a b)

So lens's over & set apply to a PartialIso as such a Prism.

>>> over (withPartialMarket (satisfied even) prism :: Prism Int (Maybe Int) Int Int) (* 2) 4
Just 8
>>> over (withPartialMarket (satisfied even) prism :: Prism Int (Maybe Int) Int Int) (* 2) 3
Nothing
>>> over (withPartialMarket _Just prism :: Prism (Maybe Int) (Maybe (Maybe Int)) Int Int) succ Nothing
Just Nothing

Only a Choice which isn't CocartesianChoice can tell passing through from failing, like (->), by traverseOf.

>>> withPartialMarket _Left $ \_ match -> (match (Left 'x'), match (Right 'y') :: Either (Maybe (Either Char Char)) Char)
(Right 'x',Left (Just (Right 'y')))

partialMarket :: (b -> Maybe t) -> (s -> Either (Maybe t) a) -> PartialIso s t a b Source #

Build a PartialIso from the functions of a PartialMarket, building backwards, partially, & matching forwards, like prism, either its focus, or on a mismatch, Just a target to pass through, or Nothing to fail.

partialIso builds a PartialIso from partial functions, whose mismatches always fail; it is partialMarket without passing through,

prop> partialIso match build = partialMarket build (maybe (Left Nothing) Right . match)

so every PartialIso is a partialMarket, but not every one a partialIso, like a Prism, which passes a mismatch through. They differ only at a Choice which can tell passing through from failing, like (->), by traverseOf.

>>> traverseOf (partialMarket (Just . Just) (maybe (Left (Just Nothing)) Right)) (Just . succ) (Nothing :: Maybe Int)
Just Nothing
>>> traverseOf (partialIso id (Just . Just)) (Just . succ) (Nothing :: Maybe Int)
Nothing

Convert APartialIso to its partial functions, matching forwards & building backwards, like withIso. They forget passing through, failing on a mismatch which a Prism passes through, so they characterize only a PartialIso which never passes through, one built by partialIso; its PartialMarket, by withPartialMarket, characterizes any.

prop> withPartialIso (partialIso match build) k = k match build

clonePartialIso :: APartialIso s t a b -> PartialIso s t a b Source #

Clone APartialIso so that you can reuse the same monomorphically typed partial isomorphism for different purposes. It keeps a Prism's passing a mismatch through, unlike rebuilding it from its partial functions, by withPartialIso pattern partialIso, which fails on a mismatch instead. They agree at every CocartesianChoice, like a parser's, which can't tell passing through from failing, & differ at a Choice which can, like (->), by traverseOf.

prop> clonePartialIso pattern = pattern
>>> traverseOf (clonePartialIso _Just) (Just . succ) Nothing
Just Nothing
>>> traverseOf (withPartialIso _Just partialIso) (Just . succ) Nothing
Nothing

coPartialIso :: APartialIso b a t s -> PartialIso s t a b Source #

Clone and invert APartialIso, by its partial functions. Only matching forwards can pass a value through, building backwards can't, so the inverse of a PartialIso which passes through fails instead. coPartialIso is its own inverse on PartialIsos which never pass through, those rebuilt from their partial functions, & inverts any other as that rebuilding.

prop> coPartialIso pattern = coPartialIso (withPartialIso pattern partialIso)
prop> coPartialIso (coPartialIso pattern) = withPartialIso pattern partialIso

crossPartialIso :: APartialIso s t a b -> APartialIso u v c d -> PartialIso (s, u) (t, v) (a, c) (b, d) Source #

Construct a PartialIso on pairs from components, passing a pair through where both components pass through, & otherwise failing on a mismatch, since a pair passed through needs a target from each component.

>>> traverseOf (crossPartialIso _Just _Just) (\(x, y) -> Just (x + y, x * y)) (Just 2, Just 3 :: Maybe Int)
Just (Just 5,Just 6)
>>> traverseOf (crossPartialIso _Just _Just) (Just . id) (Nothing, Nothing :: Maybe Int)
Just (Nothing,Nothing)
>>> traverseOf (crossPartialIso _Just _Just) (Just . id) (Just 2, Nothing :: Maybe Int)
Nothing

orPartialIso :: APartialIso s t a b -> APartialIso s t a b -> PartialIso s t a b Source #

The union of two patterns, trying the first, then the second, in either direction, & on a mismatch of both, passing through the first target passed through, by the DisjunctiveMonoid of their PartialMarkets.

>>> [traverseOf (orPartialIso (satisfied even) (satisfied odd)) Just n | n <- [2, 3 :: Int]]
[Just 2,Just 3]

N.B. >|< on APartialIsos typechecks, pointwise, but is wrong: it chooses within PartialMarket a b s (Maybe t), whose Just Nothing is a failure, which it takes for a success.

>>> [traverseOf (clonePartialIso (satisfied even >|< satisfied odd)) Just n | n <- [2, 3 :: Int]]
[Just 2,Nothing]

filterPrism :: (Profunctor p, Filterable f) => APrism b a t s -> p a (f b) -> p s (f t) Source #

A filter pattern of APrism, as the coprism ?< acts on p a (f b), total forwards & partial backwards, failing by Filterable alone. As a Prism is a constructor pattern, like Right x in a case expression, passing any other case through, a filterPrism is a filter pattern, like Right x <- xs in a comprehension or a do block, filtering any other case out, by discarding the residual of a mismatch. It runs as its Prism does, reversed, building forwards by view, as the prism reviews, & matching backwards by preview of its coPartialIso, as the prism previews.

>>> view (filterPrism _Just) 'x'
Just 'x'
>>> preview (coPartialIso (filterPrism _Just)) (Just 'x')
Just 'x'
>>> preview (coPartialIso (filterPrism _Just)) (Nothing :: Maybe Char)
Nothing

An optic for Filtrators may only map its source & filter its target, as a filterPrism does, since by its laws, a Filtrator's Cochoice derives from its Profunctor & Filterable structure, unleft = lmap Left . mapMaybe (either Just (const Nothing)).

satisfied :: (a -> Bool) -> PartialIso' a a Source #

satisfied is the prototypical proper partial isomorphism, identifying a subset which satisfies a predicate.

It is a homomorphism of BooleanAlgebras of predicates, from >|< to >|< of its partial functions, which choose by first success, in either direction, & from >&< to composition.

prop> withPartialIso (satisfied (p >|< q)) (,) = withPartialIso (satisfied p) (,) >|< withPartialIso (satisfied q) (,)
prop> withPartialIso (satisfied falseP) (,) = falseP
prop> satisfied (p >&< q) = satisfied p . satisfied q
prop> satisfied trueP = id

Template Haskell

makeNestedPrisms :: Name -> DecsQ Source #

Similar to makePrisms, makeNestedPrisms generates a Prism for each constructor of a data type. Isos are generated when possible. Reviews are generated for constructors with existentially quantified constructors and GADTs. The difference in makeNestedPrisms is that constructors with n > 2 arguments will use right-nested pairs, rather than a flat n-tuple. This makes them suitable for pattern bonding, by use of the applicator >? to Monoidal idiom notation with >*<, or to Monadic qualified do-notation.

e.g.

data FooBar a
  = Foo a
  | Bar Int
  | Baz Int Char
  | Buzz Double String Bool
  | Boop
makeNestedPrisms ''FooBar

will create

_Foo :: Prism (FooBar a) (FooBar b) a b
_Bar :: Prism' (FooBar a) Int
_Baz :: Prism' (FooBar a) (Int, Char)
_Buzz :: Prism' (FooBar a) (Double, (String, Bool))
_Boop :: Prism' (FooBar a) ()
